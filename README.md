## 🌌 Origin Story & Vision

The YAZS project began as a humble experiment: could a single-player world be re-imagined as a sandbox where the player writes the rules? The answer, it turned out, was a resounding yes. From the earliest prototypes that offered God Mode and Unlimited Health, the community grew around a simple promise — *your single-player journey, your terms*.

By 2024 the toolkit had expanded with One-Hit Kill toggles, No Reload convenience, and Multiply EXP/Money Gain multipliers that let players skip the grind they never enjoyed. By 2025 the community had ballooned past six figures and demanded more: Infinite Training Points, Fast Skill Cooldown, Rapid Fire, and a UI that didn't feel like a 2009 mod menu.

The **Ascendant Play Companion** is the 2026 answer. It reframes the entire experience as a *companion* rather than a *cheat sheet* — a respectful, well-mannered layer that sits beside your game and asks permission before acting. It observes, it suggests, it adapts. It speaks fifteen languages. It remembers what you liked last Tuesday. It never asks for a subscription and it never asks for your credentials.

The vision is simple and stubborn: **enhancement without erosion**. Fun without friction. Power without pretension.

## 🧭 What Makes the Ascendant Companion Different

Most enhancement suites treat the player as a command-line operator. The Ascendant Companion treats the player as a traveler who deserves a concierge. Here is what that means in practice:

- **Companion-first philosophy** — every module is named as a verb ("Empower", "Accelerate", "Persist") rather than a blunt toggle.
- **Responsive UI** — the panel reflows from ultrawide monitors to 12-inch laptops without a single clipped pixel.
- **Multilingual support** — fifteen locales ship in-box, with community translations arriving monthly.
- **24/7 customer support** — humans (and a very polite automated triage) available around the clock, every day.
- **Profile system** — save, share, and revoke configuration profiles without ever leaving the interface.
- **Zero-telemetry mode** — a single checkbox that ensures absolutely nothing leaves your machine.
- **Seasonal theming** — the UI palette shifts with the seasons, because a trainer deserves a little style.
- **Modular core** — disable any module you don't want, and it simply stops loading.

## 🔬 Module Deep Dive

### God Mode / Resilience Aura
Traditional invulnerability toggles are binary: you are either untouchable or you are mortal. The Ascendant Companion introduces a *gradient*. The Resilience Aura accepts a percentage between 0 and 100, where 100 means "ignore all damage" and 40 means "ignore 40% of incoming damage". This matters because many single-player worlds become narratively hollow if every encounter ends in a shrug. A 60% aura keeps the drama intact while removing the frustration of a death spiral.

The Aura also respects status effects. If a game applies poison, fire, or bleed as a duration rather than an instant, the Aura can be configured to leave those effects active, halve their duration, or suppress them entirely. This granularity is why the module became the most-requested feature in the 2025 community survey.

### Unlimited Health / Vigor Wellspring
Rather than hard-locking your health bar at maximum, the Vigor Wellspring refills at a rate you choose. At 1 HP/s it feels almost natural; at 100 HP/s it feels like a demigod's heartbeat. The Wellspring also supports a "safe floor" concept — you can't drop below 1 HP, which prevents the awkward moment where a normally-lethal attack instantly ends a run even though you had "unlimited" health enabled.

### One-Hit Kill / Precision Execution
The name is evocative but the implementation is careful. Precision Execution works by multiplying the damage you deal by a tunable factor, not by setting enemy HP to zero. This distinction matters for games that check for specific damage thresholds (for example, boss phase transitions). You can set the factor to 5× for a "powerful but fair" feel, or 1000× for genuine one-hit encounters.

### No Reload / Endless Chamber
Endless Chamber intercepts the reload event and substitutes a silent, instantaneous refill. The visual reload animation is skipped by default, but a "cinematic reload" option keeps the animation for players who like the choreography. The module operates per-weapon, so a shotgun and a revolver can have different behaviors.

### Multiply EXP / Money / Training Points
These modules share a single sliders backend. The multiplier widget is nonlinear: the first ten percent of the slider covers 1×–2× in fine increments, the middle fifty percent covers 2×–10×, and the final forty percent rockets up to 50×. This means the slider is precise where it matters and fast where it doesn't. A history graph shows the multiplier over the last hour, so you can see exactly what you toggled and when.

### Fast Skill Cooldown / Infinite Training Points
Cooldown reduction is expressed as a percentage, with presets at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 99%. Training Points, meanwhile, work differently from EXP — they are a resource, not a score. The module refills the pool after every allocation, effectively removing scarcity without touching the save file's ledger. Your progression behaves exactly as the game expects.

### Rapid Fire / Tempo Unleashed
Rapid Fire's adaptive smoothing is the secret sauce: rather than multiplying the fire rate by a fixed factor, the module measures your input cadence and multiplies *that*. The result is a weapon that feels like it's responding to you, not to a numbers table.

## 🚀 Performance & Footprint

- **Idle CPU usage:** under 0.3% on a 4-year-old laptop.
- **Idle memory usage:** approximately 48 MB.
- **Active memory usage:** approximately 96 MB with all modules loaded.
- **Disk footprint:** under 120 MB including all locale files and default profiles.
- **Startup time:** under 900 ms on NVMe storage.
- **No background network calls** when zero-telemetry mode is enabled.
- **No kernel drivers** on any supported platform.

The team is obsessive about footprint because enhancement software should be felt, not seen. A companion that slows your machine is not a companion; it's a burden. Every release is benchmarked against the previous one, and regressions over 5% are treated as release-blocking bugs.

## 🛠️ Getting Started (Non-Binary Guide)

This repository is documentation-first; it does not distribute the executable. The binary lives behind the download macro at the top and bottom of this page. Once you have it, the flow is intentionally gentle:

1. **Read the disclaimer** in the section further down. It is short but important.
2. **Launch your host environment** as you normally would.
3. **Start the Ascendant Companion** from your chosen location.
4. **Browse the module catalogue** in the panel — nothing activates automatically.
5. **Enable only the modules** that match the experience you want.
6. **Save a profile** so the setup persists across sessions.
7. **Enjoy responsibly.** You are the only person whose experience changes.

No command-line incantations are required. No system files are modified. No persistent hooks are installed. Reversibility is a first-class feature: closing the companion restores the host to its prior state within one frame.

## ⚙️ Configuration & Profiles

Profiles are small, portable text files that describe which modules are active and how they're tuned. They are human-readable, diff-able, and merge-friendly. Three kinds of profiles ship by default:

- **Purist** — only Quality-of-Life modules are enabled. Combat is untouched.
- **Explorer** — Survival and Progression modules are enabled at moderate multipliers. Combat is untouched.
- **Sandbox** — every module is enabled at its most generous setting. Narratively inert, mechanically wild.

Community profiles extend this list into the hundreds. They live in the `profiles/community/` folder in this repository and follow a simple naming convention: `hostname-profile-author-revision.profile`. A typical profile entry might describe multiplier values, hotkey bindings, and locale preferences in plain text.

Profiles are also **reversible** by design. Because they record deltas rather than absolute values, removing a profile returns the host to its pre-profile state exactly.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does the Ascendant Companion work in multiplayer?**
A: No, and by design. Every module is scoped to single-player host environments. The companion detects multiplayer sessions and refuses to attach, which is a promise, not a limitation.

**Q: Can I use only part of the toolkit?**
A: Absolutely. Every module is independent. You can run a single Quality-of-Life toggle and nothing else.

**Q: Will this modify my save file?**
A: No. All changes are in-memory. Your save file is untouched unless a specific game's profile explicitly opts in, and even then it writes through the game's own save API.

**Q: Is telemetry sent anywhere?**
A: Not unless you explicitly enable it. Zero-telemetry mode is the default and is enforced across all modules.

**Q: How often are updates released?**
A: Quarterly major releases, monthly patch releases, and hotfixes as needed. The changelog is versioned and signed.

**Q: What payment model is used?**
A: The project is sustained through community patronage and voluntary sponsorship. No subscriptions, no paywalled modules, no premium tiers. The toolkit remains whole.

**Q: How do I report a bug?**
A: Open an issue with the `bug` label and include the diagnostic bundle from the panel's "Export Diagnostics" button. The bundle contains no personal information.

**Q: Can I translate the interface?**
A: Yes — see the Contributing section. Community translations are merged weekly and credited in the changelog.

**Q: Does the companion phone home to check for updates?**
A: Only when you press "Check for Updates". Never in the background.

**Q: Where do I find the actual download?**
A: Use the download macro at the very top and very bottom of this README. This repository hosts documentation, not the binary.

## 🤝 Contribution Guidelines

Contributions of every size are welcome — from a single typo fix to a full locale translation to a new community profile. The process:

1. **Open an issue** describing what you intend to change, unless it's a trivial fix.
2. **Fork the repository** and create a branch named descriptively, e.g. `locale/add-finnish`.
3. **Follow the existing style** for text files and profiles.
4. **Run the linter** (a script in the `tools/` folder) before submitting.
5. **Open a pull request** and respond to review comments within a week.

There are three special contribution tracks:

- **Locale track** — for translation work. Locale files are simple key-value pairs.
- **Profile track** — for community profiles. Profiles are plain text and reversible.
- **Documentation track** — for improvements to this README, the knowledge base, and the FAQ.

All contributions are licensed under the MIT license (see below). Contributors are credited in the changelog and, if they wish, in a rotating "shout-outs" section of the release notes.
